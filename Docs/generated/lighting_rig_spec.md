# XCOM Reveal Lighting Rig - UE5 Handoff (v4)

Generated by `Tools/blender/rig_evidence.py` from the measured Blender
renders. Every intensity below was taken from the rig that produced the
reference frames in `CommandCenter/outputs/lighting/`.

**Unit conversion used:** UE5 candela = 7.96 x Blender watts for a
point/spot.  (Blender renders linear 1.0 = white; UE5 at EV100 treats
linear 1.0 = 100 cd; a Blender point/spot of P W has radial radiant
intensity P/(4*pi) W/sr, so I_UE = 100 * P/(4*pi) = 7.96 * P.)  If the
project uses a different exposure, scale every intensity by the same
factor and re-check against the reference PNGs.

**Actor convention:** the reference rig was authored on a 1.028 m tall
body.  In UE the spawn actor is scaled to 1.8 m tall; all transforms
below are expressed RELATIVE to the actor root at the feet, in UE axes
(X forward = the direction the creature faces, Y right, Z up).

Rotation note: a UE spotlight emits along -Z at zero rotation, so the

equivalent and is usually easier to wire in Blueprint.

### 1a. reveal_key - hard cold key, front-left

- Type: **Spot**, colour (0.62, 0.78, 1.00), intensity **84.1 cd**
- Inner cone 24 deg / outer cone 40 deg, shadow casting ON.
- Location (actor space): (1.81, -1.688, 3.488)   Aim at chest: (0.0, 0.0, 1.26)
- Rotation: (-131.99, 137.0, 0.0)
- Front-left and ABOVE the creature, aimed down at the chest.  This is
  the light that throws the cast shadow; it is the only light whose
  shadow direction matters for the composition.

### 1b. reveal_rim - alien green rim, behind-above

The reference rig uses a LONG THIN AREA STRIP (5.00 x 1.20 body
heights) behind and above the subject.  A strip wraps the whole
silhouette edge in one pass; a spot only ever grazes a thin slice of
it, and trying to make a spot cover the edge is what drives the rim
into the thousands of watts and blows the frame out.

In UE use a **Rect Area Light** with the same size and orientation, or
a Spot with a wide cone if the project has no area lights.  The
intensity below is the measured Blender value; for UE, divide by the
strip area and use the resulting value with a wide outer cone.

- Type: **Spot**, colour (0.20, 1.00, 0.42), intensity **2942.9 cd**
- Inner cone 30 deg / outer cone 50 deg, shadow casting ON.
- Location (actor space): (-1.454, -0.839, 2.309)   Aim at chest: (0.0, 0.0, 1.26)
- Rotation: (-122.0, 29.99, 0.0)
- Behind and above, grazing the silhouette.  It is the SEPARATION light:
  with the backdrop near-black, this is the only thing that draws the
  outline.  Never let it be culled or replaced by a fill.

### 1c. reveal_under - cyan under-glow, local uplight

- Type: **Point**, colour (0.45, 0.90, 1.00), intensity **2942.9 cd**
- Location (actor space): (0.108, 0.0, 0.072)  (at the feet, between them, 0.07 m above
  the floor)  Aim at the legs: (0.0, 0.0, 0.18)
- Attenuation radius 2 cm, shadow casting OFF.
- CRITICAL: this must stay a tight local uplight.  A wide or bright
  version floods the floor and the frame goes from "creature in the
  dark" to "product shot on a white light box".  If the under-glow
  needs to read further, raise the intensity, never the radius.

### 1d. reveal_pool + reveal_wash - the floor

- reveal_pool: **Spot**, colour (0.52, 0.72, 1.00), intensity **247.3 cd**,
  cone 22 deg with a soft edge, aimed at the ground 0.63 m in front of
  the feet, from behind-above.  It makes the bounded pool of light on
  the floor AND the creature's own contact shadow inside it.
- reveal_wash: **Point**, colour (0.45, 0.55, 0.70), intensity **19.8 cd**,
  25 cm source radius, shadow casting off.  This is the only ambient
  term in the rig.  It must stay dim: it exists so the shadow side of
  the floor is readable, not so the creature is lit from nowhere.

## 2. Emissive materials

| Material | Emission colour | Blender emission strength | UE5 emissive value |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| `XCOM_Chryssalid_ACCENT_EMISSIVE` | (0.05, 1.00, 0.25) | 0.50 | 10.0 |

UE5 multiplies an emissive colour by 20 internally, so the UE column is
the Blender strength x 20.  Set these on the material instance; do not
bake them into the base material.

The proof frames in `CommandCenter/outputs/lighting/` were rendered with
**no scene lights at all** and the emissive strength multiplied by
6.0, plus a Fog Glow compositor pass.  That is the evidence that the
emissives genuinely self-light.

## 3. World, fog and post

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---------|-------|-----|
| World / Sky light | None, or intensity 0.02 with a cold tint | The backdrop must fall away so the rim does the separating. |
| Sky atmosphere | Off | Any sky fill re-introduces the "fakes the green material" failure. |
| Exponential Height Fog | Fog Density **0.012**, Height Falloff **0.25**, Start Distance 0 | Just enough haze for the under-glow and pool to read volumetrically. |
| Directional Inscattering Colour | (0.06, 0.12, 0.20) | Cold haze tint, matches the key. |
| Inscattering Exponent | 4.0 | Keeps the haze off the camera. |
| Volumetric Fog Scattering | 0.04 | The light shafts in the pool need it. |
| Volumetric Fog Distance | 8000 cm | |
| Bloom (post) | Intensity 0.25, Threshold 0.85, Radius 4 | Matches the Blender Fog Glow used for the reference frames. |
| Auto exposure | OFF, fixed EV100 | The rig is authored at a fixed exposure; auto exposure would blow the pool out and black out the backdrop. |

Fog is what makes the reveal volumetric: without it the under-glow and
the pool are flat discs on a flat plane.  Keep the density low - the
reference is a dark scene, not a smoky one.

## 4. Consistency rules for the whole set

- The key is ALWAYS front-left.  The rig is authored subject-relative,
  so all five enemies cast their shadow the same way.
- The rim is ALWAYS behind-above.
- The world stays near-black (Blender background strength 0.10 in the
  reference renders, which measured a background_darkness of 71.3).
- Nothing in the rig may be allowed to clip: the reference frames have
  0 clipped white pixels on the body.

## 6. Wiring it up in Blueprint

1. Add a `BP_XCOM_RevealRig` actor component with five child components:
   `SpotLight reveal_key`, `SpotLight reveal_rim`, `PointLight reveal_under`,
   `SpotLight reveal_pool`, `PointLight reveal_wash`.
2. Set the transforms from section 1 (they are already actor-relative).
3. On `BeginPlay`, disable the whole rig.  On the reveal event, enable
   `reveal_key`, `reveal_rim`, `reveal_pool`, `reveal_wash` and enable
   `reveal_under` with a 0.4 s fade-in while the creature rises.
4. `reveal_under` off, `reveal_key`/`reveal_rim` stay on for the rest of
   the encounter.
